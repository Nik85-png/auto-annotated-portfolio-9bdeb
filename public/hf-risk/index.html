Predicting heart failure outcomes with leakage-clean machine learning.

HF-RISK trains models on 2,008 hospitalised heart failure patients from the Zhang / PhysioNet cohort, then tests transfer to 42,990 MIMIC-IV patients. The final results are deliberately conservative: administrative IDs, discharge timing, and post-outcome leakage variables were removed before modelling.

What the project does

The project predicts four discharge outcomes: death within 28 days, death within 3 months, death within 6 months, and readmission within 6 months. The main models are trained on the Chinese Zhang cohort and internally tested on a held-out Zhang test set.

The work then asks a harder question: can those same Zhang-trained models transfer to MIMIC-IV, a US hospital dataset with different patients, coding, missingness, and available measurements?

Why the numbers are conservative

The first models looked stronger and appeared competitive with several published studies. A later audit found that some signal came from administrative, temporal, or post-outcome proxy variables. Those variables were removed, which lowered AUROC but made the methodology more defensible.

Model audit: why the final AUROC is lower

The project deliberately reports the stricter leakage-clean results rather than the most flattering early numbers. That choice is the point: the final model is easier to defend in an academic or clinical review.

Before audit Higher AUROC

Early models reached stronger headline scores and appeared to beat several benchmarks, but the feature set still contained variables that could act as shortcuts.

Audit finding Leakage risk

Patient identifiers, discharge timing, and post-discharge outcome proxies were identified as unfair or fragile predictors for a real discharge-time model.

Final result Honest model

After removing those variables, the primary 6-month mortality AUROC became 0.710 internally and 0.599 on MIMIC-IV, giving conservative but defensible evidence.

MIMIC-IV external validation

External validation applies the Zhang-trained models directly to MIMIC-IV. No retraining happens in this step.

28-day Mortality 0.524 MIMIC-IV external AUROC, 95% CI 0.515-0.532
6-month Mortality 0.599 MIMIC-IV external AUROC, 95% CI 0.593-0.606

Interpretation

External AUROC is lower than internal AUROC, especially for 28-day mortality. This is evidence of dataset shift: the US MIMIC cohort differs in population, measurement availability, coding, and missingness. This does not invalidate the project; it is a major finding about clinical model transportability.
