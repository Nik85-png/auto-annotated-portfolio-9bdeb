Research tool

Streamlit risk prototype

The tool loads the final trained models, fills missing inputs with training medians, maps readable feature names to model-safe names, and returns predicted risk for each outcome.

  • ModelUses the final 143-feature leakage-clean model set.
  • OutputShows four outcomes: 28-day death, 3-month death, 6-month death, and 6-month readmission.
  • SafetyTesting and demonstration only. Not clinically validated.

The project story

The page now follows the actual research process: build, audit, explain, test externally, and package as a research prototype.

Develop

Train models on the Zhang / PhysioNet heart failure cohort using 2,008 hospitalised patients.

Audit

Remove patient ID, discharge timing, and post-outcome proxies that could inflate performance.

Explain

Use SHAP to identify clinically plausible drivers such as GCS, CKD, valve measures, and liver disease.

Validate

Apply Zhang-trained models directly to MIMIC-IV to measure external transportability.

The audit changed the result

Before the strict audit, AUROC looked strong enough to support a simple "beats benchmarks" story. The audit found that some of this strength came from variables that are not defensible for a true discharge-time model. Reporting the reduced AUROC is a sign of methodological integrity, not failure.

External validation on MIMIC-IV

Here the Zhang-trained models are applied directly to MIMIC-IV. They are not retrained. The drop is evidence of dataset shift and limited transportability.

28-day Mortality 0.524 MIMIC-IV external AUROC, 95% CI 0.515-0.532
6-month Mortality 0.599 MIMIC-IV external AUROC, 95% CI 0.593-0.606

How to read this

Internal performance asks whether the model works on unseen Zhang patients. External validation asks the harder question: can a Zhang-trained model work in a different US hospital system? The answer is: only partly. That is an important finding for clinical ML deployment.

Important safety note

This is a dissertation research prototype. It is not a medical device, not clinically validated, and not suitable for patient-care decisions. The value of the tool is methodological demonstration: feature handling, model loading, prediction output, and explainability workflow.
